# My open source history

Git history makes every commit look the same. Mine spans twenty years and four
very different ways of working, and I think the differences matter more than
the totals. So here is my open source record with the attribution stated
plainly.

## 2001–2008: hand-written, in community

At IBM I was a key architect of the **Web Services Invocation Framework
(WSIF)** and wrote its first code. WSIF was donated to Apache (now retired to
the Attic).

In 2005 I co-founded **Apache Synapse**, the lightweight ESB/mediation engine,
and wrote a substantial part of the early codebase — 165 commits between
October 2005 and June 2008, still visible in the project history. Synapse
became the engine inside WSO2's integration products, where its descendants
still run today.

I also made a small number of commits to **Apache Axis2** in 2007. I was part
of that community, but Axis2 was built by others — I won't claim more than the
handful of commits the log shows.

## 2005–2016: architect and sponsor, not author

As co-founder and CTO of **WSO2** I had a leadership role in products that
have had real reach — **API Manager**, **Identity Server**, the **ESB/Enterprise
Integrator** line (built on Synapse), the **Carbon** platform, **Siddhi**, and
later **Ballerina**. I helped shape architecture, made technology bets, and
ran the engineering organisation for parts of that time.

To be clear: the code in those repositories was written by WSO2's engineers,
not me. There are no commits of mine in those trees, and the credit for the
craftsmanship belongs to the teams. My contribution was direction, design
input, and building the company that built them. Ballerina in particular was
driven by Sanjiva Weerawarana; I was a supporter and occasional adviser, not a designer or direct contributor. My role was more about developer relations and evangelism.

## 2015–2021: hand-written, solo

My PhD work on federated identity for IoT produced **OAuthing**, a research
prototype — solo-authored, and dormant since the thesis. It moved the dial on privacy and identity in IoT networks.

Alongside it I wrote course materials for teaching at Oxford (software engineering, SOA, big data).
These were one-person projects published for the benefit of students and consistently received excellent student feedback. They have not been maintained since I retired from that role.

## 2025–present: agentic, solo

Everything I have published since 2025 — **estampo** (reproducible 3D-print
builds), **build123d-mcp** (an MCP server for AI-assisted CAD),
**wormgear**, **overstand**, and the rest of the 3D agentic tooling cluster — was
built with agentic coding: AI agents writing code under my direction, review,
and testing.

The commits carry my name, but my role is closer to specifier, reviewer, and
product owner than line-by-line author. I think this is worth saying out
loud: it is a genuinely new mode of working, the git metadata doesn't
distinguish it from hand-written code, and pretending otherwise would
misrepresent the work. What I stand behind is the design, the correctness,
and the usefulness of these tools — not the keystrokes.

## Overview

Each era taught me something different: writing code in a community taught me
software development; running WSO2 taught me what it takes to sustain it at scale; the PhD years developed my research and teaching skills; and the agentic era is exploring 
what software authorship means when the typing is no longer the bottleneck.
